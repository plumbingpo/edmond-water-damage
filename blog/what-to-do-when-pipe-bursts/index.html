Burst Pipe Emergency Guide in Edmond, OK

Home / Blog / What to Do When a Pipe Bursts in Edmond, OK

A burst pipe is one of the most stressful plumbing emergencies a homeowner can experience. Water pours out of a burst line at a rate of 4 to 8 gallons a minute, so within hours rooms may be flooded, possessions damaged, and building materials weakened. Knowing exactly what to do in the first critical minutes can save you thousands of dollars in damage and separate a manageable repair from a major restoration project. Edmond Restoration put together these steps so Edmond, OK residents know exactly how to respond to this common problem.

Step 1: Turn the Water Off Right Away

Before anything else, cut off the water, because nothing you do afterward matters as much as stopping the flow quickly. Every minute the water keeps flowing adds to the damage and the cost. Here's what to do: locate your main water shutoff valve. Homes in Edmond usually have it close to the meter: check the basement, the garage, a utility room, or an outside wall facing the street. Turn the valve clockwise (righty-tighty) to shut off water flow to the entire house. When the broken pipe is easy to spot and has a dedicated valve close by, shut that one too.

Can't find the valve, or can't get it to turn? Phone the water utility right away and ask them to stop service at the meter. It is also why every homeowner should learn where the shutoff is long before anything goes wrong. Take a few minutes today to locate yours and make sure it operates smoothly. A valve that will not move should be repaired by a plumber now, not during a flood.

Step 3: Protect Your Property and Belongings

There is useful work to do until help shows up: first, carry anything valuable, plus paperwork and electronics, to a dry room. Furniture resting on damp carpet should be raised, with foil or plastic blocks set under each leg. Pick up any area rugs lying on wet flooring. Use towels to soak up whatever standing water you can reach. Air needs to circulate, so leave cabinets and closets open. If you can reach the breaker panel safely and stay out of the water, switch off power to the affected rooms.

Never walk through standing water near electrical outlets, appliances, or panels. Can't get to the breaker panel safely because of serious flooding? Leave the power as it is until trained help arrives and assesses things. Your safety matters more than the house.

Step 4: Take Photos Before You Clean Up

Your insurance claim will need evidence, so record everything carefully before any cleaning starts. Take photos and video of all standing water, damaged materials, and affected belongings. Write down when you found the break, both the date and the rough time, and photograph the leak's source if you can see it. This documentation, combined with the detailed reports Edmond Restoration provides, will support your insurance claim and help you seek fair compensation for the damage.

Preventing Burst Pipes in Edmond

Some bursts cannot be avoided, but Edmond homeowners lower the odds a great deal when they insulate exposed pipes in unheated spots such as attics, crawl spaces, and garages. When temperatures drop, let faucets drip a little so water keeps moving in the lines. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls to allow warm air to reach pipes. Keep an eye on water pressure, and if it runs above 80 PSI, have a pressure reducing valve put in. Braided stainless steel should take the place of worn supply lines, rubber washing machine hoses above all. And know your shutoff valve location before you need it.
